## 1. IFCN Code of Principles, commitment 3

Source: https://ifcncodeofprinciples.poynter.org/the-commitments (read 2026-11-07).

Commitment text, quoted:

- "Signatory organizations are transparent about their funding sources."
- "If they accept funding from other organizations, they ensure that funders have no influence over the conclusions the fact-checkers reach in their reports."
- "Signatory organizations detail the professional background of all key figures in the organization and explain the organizational structure and legal status."
- "Signatories clearly indicate a way for readers to communicate with them."

Assessment criteria:

- **3.1 Funding (independent organizations):** "Applicants that are independent organizations have a page on their website detailing each source of funding accounting for 5% or more of total revenue for its previous financial year. This page also sets out the legal form in which the organization is registered (e.g. as a non-profit, as a company, etc)."
- **3.2 Ownership:** "Applicants that are the fact-checking section or unit of a media house or other parent organization make a statement on ownership."
- **3.3 Structure:** a statement "sets out the applicant's organizational structure and makes clear how and by whom editorial control is exercised."
- **3.4 Staff:** a page "details the professional biography of all those who, according to the organization structure, play a significant part in its editorial output."
- **3.5 Contact:** "easy means on its website and/or via social media for users to communicate with the editorial team."

Eligibility (outside commitment 3, same page): status "may not be granted to organizations whose editorial work is controlled by the state, a party or politician", but may be granted to organizations funded from state or political sources "if the IFCN assessor determines there is clear and unambiguous separation of editorial control".

Points to note:

- The IFCN rule is organization-level (a page on the website) and annual (previous financial year). It is not a per-article statement.
- The threshold is a share of revenue (5%), not a dollar figure. Small donors fall below it automatically.
- There is no exemption for in-kind support in the text; the text says "each source of funding" (whether in-kind counts is not stated; see Not verified).

## 2. Journalism and nonprofit news

### INN (Institute for Nonprofit News)

inn.org returned HTTP 403 to this session. The text below is INN's standard as reproduced by member outlets.

- Voice of San Diego funding policy PDF (https://voiceofsandiego.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/01/Voice-of-San-Diego-Funding-Policy.pdf, read 2026-11-07) states that it "subscribes to the Institute for Nonprofit News' standards of editorial independence as follows":
  - "retains full authority over editorial content ... We will maintain a firewall between news coverage decisions and sources of all revenue."
  - "Acceptance of financial support does not constitute implied or actual endorsement of donors or their products, services or opinions."
  - "We accept gifts, grants and sponsorships ... for the general support of our operations, but our news judgments are made independently and not on the basis of donor support."
  - may accept "donations to support the coverage of particular topics, but our organization maintains editorial control of the coverage. We will cede no right of review or influence of editorial content".
  - "will make public all donors who financially support us".
- Capitol News Illinois (https://capitolnewsillinois.com/ABOUT-US/Editorial-Independence-Policy, read 2026-11-07): "will make public all donors who give a total of $5,000 or more per year"; anonymous donations "for general support only" with safeguards; "We will cede no right of review or influence of editorial content."
- The Recorder (https://www.therecorder.org/donorlisting, read 2026-11-07): discloses "donors contributing $5,000 or more annually, with donor approval"; "accepts anonymous donations that in aggregate do not exceed 15% of total donations" (12% in 2024).
- Search summaries of inn.org (not fetched) say INN now recommends posting "all major revenue sources, not just donors" (including government funding, prompted by PPP loans) and that, in the older membership criteria, fiscally sponsored members could link to the sponsor's Form 990 and list funders of $5,000 and above on their own site. **Not verified on inn.org.**

### SPJ Code of Ethics

Source: https://www.spj.org/spj-code-of-ethics/ (read 2026-11-07). The page says: "Revised October 4, 2026 at SPJ's National Convention in Columbus, Ohio."

"Act Independently", quoted: "Avoid conflicts of interest, real or perceived. Disclose, explain and seek to lessen the effect of unavoidable conflicts." "Refuse presents, favors, fees, free travel or special treatment aimed at unduly influencing coverage." "Deny favored treatment to advertisers, sources, donors or other special interests." "Distinguish news from advertising and shun hybrids that blur the lines between the two. Prominently label sponsored content and partnerships."

"Be Accountable and Transparent": "Clearly identify the sources and technology that shape the creation of news. The public is entitled to as much detail as possible to judge the reliability and motives of sources."

### The Trust Project

Source: https://thetrustproject.org/trust-indicators/ (read 2026-11-07). Indicator 1, "Best Practices", asks readers: "Does the site use guidelines to protect honest and independent reporting? What is the site's mission and priorities? Who funds them?" The Trust Project's own funding statement: "Trust Project policies and the Trust Indicators are shaped and enforced independently from our funding sources."

Machine-readable markup (schema.org V30.1, 2026-09-16; read 2026-11-07):

- https://schema.org/ownershipFundingInfo: "a description of organizational ownership structure; funding and grants. In a news/media setting, this is with particular reference to editorial independence." Used on Organization / NewsMediaOrganization. Based on The Trust Project's work.
- https://schema.org/funder: "A person or organization that supports (sponsors) something through some kind of financial contribution." Used on CreativeWork.
- https://schema.org/funding: "A Grant that directly or indirectly provide funding or sponsorship for this item." Used on CreativeWork.

### ProPublica

- Supporters (https://www.propublica.org/about/supporters, read 2026-11-07): "Following is a list of just some of our larger donors". No threshold stated.
- Code of Ethics (https://www.propublica.org/code-of-ethics, read 2026-11-07): "If we become aware that a subject of a story is also a ProPublica donor, we disclose that relationship in the story."

### CalMatters

- About (https://calmatters.org/about/, read 2026-11-07): "We are funded by a mix of foundations, major donors, members and corporate sponsors. We disclose our supporters and post our tax returns and financial statements." "The newsroom is insulated from donors, sponsors and advertisers".
- Funding (https://calmatters.org/about/funding/, read 2026-11-07): Publisher's Circle lists those giving "$10,000 or more in the past year"; a "$1,000 to $9,999" tier; "Advertisers and Sponsors of $5,000 or more from the past 12 months"; sponsors "understand that they do not influence our journalism".

### Voice of San Diego

About (https://voiceofsandiego.org/about-us/, read 2026-11-07): "The donors, sponsors, grantmakers, partners, board members and thousands of individuals who support this service financially do not influence day-to-day coverage, stories or investigations."

**Common rule across these sources:** funders get no review of the work and no influence over it; acceptance is not endorsement; restricted (topic) gifts are allowed but editorial control stays with the publisher; donor names are published at a threshold; anonymous money is kept small.

## 3. Research and advocacy

### ICMJE

Source: https://www.icmje.org/recommendations/browse/roles-and-responsibilities/author-responsibilities--conflicts-of-interest.html (read 2026-11-07). Articles should be published with statements declaring:

- "Sources of support for the work, including sponsor names along with explanations of the role of those sources if any in study design; collection, analysis, and interpretation of data; writing of the report; any restrictions regarding the submission of the report for publication; or a statement declaring that the supporting source had no such involvement or restrictions regarding publication"; and
- "Whether the authors had access to the study data".

Also: authors "must avoid entering into agreements with study sponsors ... that interfere with authors' access to all of the study's data or ... their ability to analyze and interpret the data and to prepare and publish manuscripts independently". For sponsors with a financial interest in the outcome, editors may ask for: "I had full access to all of the data in this study and I take complete responsibility for the integrity of the data and the accuracy of the data analysis."

Disclosure form (https://www.icmje.org/disclosure-of-interest/, "Updated February 2021"). Item 1 wording from completed copies of the form (secondary, via search): "All support for the present manuscript (e.g., funding, provision of study materials, medical writing, article processing charges, etc.)", "No time limit for this item"; other items cover the past 36 months. **The wording of the form was not read on icmje.org.** Note that it treats in-kind items (materials, writing help) as support.

Common journal template (generic, not from a single source): "This work was funded by [funder] (grant [no.]). The funder had no role in study design, data collection and analysis, decision to publish, or preparation of the manuscript."

### Transparify (think tanks)

Transparify's site (transparify.org) returned 404 for its methodology pages. Source used: Transparify 2016 report, "How Transparent are Think Tanks about Who Funds Them 2016", copy at https://www.corruptionwatch.org.za/wp-content/uploads/2016/07/Transparify2016ThinkTanksReport.pdf (read 2026-11-07):

- 5 stars, "highly transparent: all donors listed, clearly identifying funding amounts for, and sources of, particular projects".
- 4 stars, "broadly transparent: all donors above USD 5,000 listed in 4+ precise funding brackets, with anonymous donors no more than 15%".
- 3 stars: "all or most donors listed in 2 or 3 broad contribution brackets".
- "Organizations may exceptionally list privacy-minded donors as 'anonymous', but in order to qualify as transparent, an organization needs to disclose the sources of over 85% of its funding volume."
- Search summaries (not verified) say one star is deducted when information is more than two years out of date.

### EU Transparency Register

From a copy of Annex II hosted by the Bulgarian Parliament (https://old.parliament.bg/pub/ECD/2415401_EN_annexe_autre_acte_part1_v5.pdf), via search, not fetched: registrants such as NGOs and think tanks give funding by category, and "the amount of each contribution exceeding 10 % of the total budget, if the contributions are above 10 000 euros, and the name of the contributor". **Not verified on the official register site; the current version may differ.** This is relevant as a model of a share-plus-floor threshold.

## 4. US civic and regulatory context

### CPUC intervenor compensation (California energy proceedings)

- Program: Public Utilities Code sections 1801 to 1812, started in 1981. Awards cover the reasonable costs of intervenors who make a "substantial contribution". The utility pays and recovers the cost from ratepayers; awards in industry-wide proceedings come from ratepayer fees (CPUC program page via search: https://webproda.cpuc.ca.gov/proceedings-and-rulemaking/intervenor-compensation; not fetched directly, because the cpuc.ca.gov URL tried returned 404).
- NOI Form A, "Revised March 2023", as filed by TURN in A.25-07-016 (https://docs.cpuc.ca.gov/PublishedDocs/Efile/G000/M586/K129/586129363.PDF, read 2026-11-07):
  - Category 3 customer: "a formally organized group authorized, by its articles of incorporation or bylaws to represent the interests of residential customers or small commercial customers". It must give the percentage of members who are residential ratepayers and attach its articles or bylaws.
  - "Do you have any direct economic interest in outcomes of the proceeding? If 'Yes', explain".
  - "Conflict of Interest (§ 1802.3)".
  - Hardship, quoting § 1802(h): "The customer cannot afford, without undue hardship, to pay the costs of effective participation" or "In the case of a group or organization, the economic interest of the individual members of the group or organization is small in comparison to the costs of effective participation in the proceeding."
- An older CPUC opinion (https://docs.cpuc.ca.gov/published/Report/84455.htm, via search) provides confidentiality undertakings for an intervenor's personal financial information.
- **Finding:** this session did not find any CPUC rule that requires intervenors to publish their general funders. Disclosure is about eligibility, economic interest and conflicts. For Civicsmith, the relevant point is the reverse: if a group (or a member) receives intervenor compensation or utility money for related work, readers need to know.

### Courts: amicus briefs (closest analogue to "who paid for this piece of work")

- Supreme Court Rule 37.6 (https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/supct/rule_37, read 2026-11-07): a brief "shall indicate whether counsel for a party authored the brief in whole or in part and whether such counsel or a party made a monetary contribution intended to fund the preparation or submission of the brief, and shall identify every person or entity, other than the amicus curiae, its members, or its counsel, who made such a monetary contribution ... The disclosure shall be made in the first footnote on the first page of text."
- FRAP 29(a)(4)(E) (https://www.law.cornell.edu/rules/frap/rule_29, read 2026-11-07) has the same test; the committee note says "A party's or counsel's payment of general membership dues to an amicus need not be disclosed."
- Pending change: a FRAP 29 amendment package is on track for 1 December 2026 if the Supreme Court adopted it. On 26 March 2026 the Judicial Conference withdrew the part that would have recodified contributor disclosure (earlier drafts would have named contributors over $100) (NTU Foundation, https://www.ntu.org/foundation/detail/us-courts-withdraw-disastrous-donor-privacy-rule-change-for-amicus-filers, 30 March 2026, read 2026-11-07). **The final content of the package was not verified.**

### FTC Endorsement Guides (paid and in-kind relationships)

16 CFR 255.5 (eCFR current, read 2026-11-07): "When there exists a connection between the endorser and the seller of the advertised product that might materially affect the weight or credibility of the endorsement, and that connection is not reasonably expected by the audience, such connection must be disclosed clearly and conspicuously. Material connections can include a business, family, or personal relationship. They can include monetary payment or the provision of free or discounted products ... Some connections may be immaterial because they are too insignificant to affect the weight or credibility given to endorsements." This applies to advertising, not to Civicsmith cases, but it gives the test for in-kind support: free or discounted products are a connection, and disclosure is needed when the connection is material and the audience would not expect it.

### Comments to agencies

This session found no general US federal or California rule requiring commenters on agency rulemakings to disclose who funded their comments (not exhaustively searched; see Not verified).

## 5. Privacy, small donors, in-kind and fiscal sponsorship

- **Small donors, why $5,000:** IRS Schedule B instructions (https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i990sb, read 2026-11-07): the general rule is to report "contributions from all persons who contribute $5,000 or more (in money or other property) during the tax year". For most filers, "the names and addresses of contributors aren't required to be made available for public inspection". Amounts stay public "unless it clearly identifies the contributor". This is the probable origin of the $5,000 thresholds used by INN and Transparify.
- **Constitutional limit (California):** Americans for Prosperity Foundation v. Bonta, No. 19-251, decided 1 July 2021 (https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/20pdf/19-251_p86b.pdf, read 2026-11-07): California required charities to give the Attorney General their major donors' identities; the Court held "California's disclosure requirement is facially invalid because it burdens donors' First Amendment rights and is not narrowly tailored to an important government interest." Lesson: compelling the naming of individual supporters is sensitive; a product should not force it, and should let a group decline to name small individual supporters.
- **Anonymous money:** INN members accept anonymous gifts for general support only, capped at 15% in practice; Transparify requires at least 85% of funding volume identified; The Recorder lists donors only "with donor approval".
- **Volunteer time and in-kind:** Form 990 instructions (https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i990, read 2026-11-07): donated services or use of equipment, materials or facilities may be described in the program narrative, but "don't include these amounts in revenue, expenses, or grants". Noncash property gifts are reported on Schedule B Part II. ICMJE counts "provision of study materials, medical writing" as support (secondary). FTC treats free or discounted products as material connections. Taken together: volunteer time is normally described, not valued; donated goods and services (software, AI credits) are disclosed when they matter to credibility, especially when the donor has an interest in the subject.
- **Fiscal sponsorship:** INN's older criteria (via search, not verified) let fiscally sponsored members link to the sponsor's Form 990 and list their own funders of $5,000 and above. Model: name the fiscal sponsor and still list the project's own funders.
- **Funder as subject:** ProPublica discloses in the story when a subject is a donor; SPJ says to disclose unavoidable conflicts. This per-item rule matters more for a case than any annual list.

## 6. Synthesis for Civicsmith

Civicsmith cases are per-matter, made by small volunteer groups, and often about utilities, agencies or contractors that could themselves be funders. The best fit combines IFCN's organization-level rule (share threshold, legal form, no influence) with the per-item models (ICMJE role statement, amicus "who paid for this work", ProPublica funder-as-subject).

### Minimal honest funding statement on each published case

1. **Who paid for this work** (one choice, or a short list):
   - "The members did this work in their own time, at their own expense." (volunteer time is described, not valued)
   - "The group's own general funds" plus the group's legal form (unincorporated association, nonprofit, fiscally sponsored project of [sponsor]).
   - Named funders whose money paid for this case, or who give the group at or above a threshold (suggest: IFCN's 5% of the group's previous-year revenue; optionally with a floor such as the EU's 10%/EUR 10,000 or INN's $5,000 for larger groups). Restricted (topic) money for this case is always named, whatever the amount (amicus rule).
   - In-kind support that is material: AI service accounts or credits, donated software, paid data, legal help, with the provider named. Ordinary paid subscriptions the members buy themselves can be stated as "paid by members".
   - **"None"** is a valid answer: "No outside funding or in-kind support."
2. **Independence statement:** "No funder or provider reviewed, shaped or approved this case's findings or conclusions," adapted from ICMJE ("no such involvement or restrictions regarding publication") and IFCN ("no influence over the conclusions"). If a funder did have a role, say what it was instead (ICMJE).
3. **Funder or provider as subject:** if any funder, in-kind provider or member has a financial interest in the matter (including intervenor compensation or utility funding for related work), say so in the case (ProPublica, SPJ, CPUC "direct economic interest").
4. **Pointer to the group page:** a link to the group's standing funding page (IFCN 3.1 to 3.5: sources at or above the threshold, legal form, structure and who ratifies, contact).
5. **Technology:** the AI tools used, by name (SPJ 2026 "sources and technology"); this may live in another case block, but it should be cross-referenced when AI access was donated.
6. **Machine-readable:** schema.org `funder` / `funding` on the case; `ownershipFundingInfo` on the group.

### Optional (good, not required)

- Amounts or brackets (Transparify 4-star style) rather than names alone.
- A rolling 12-month window (CalMatters) instead of the previous financial year.
- Listing all supporters who consent (The Recorder: "with donor approval").
- An anonymous-share figure ("anonymous gifts were X% of funds").

### Overreach for volunteer groups (avoid)

- Requiring names of small individual donors or of members who chip in; IRS keeps these names private, and AFPF v. Bonta shows the sensitivity. Use "individual members and supporters" below the threshold.
- Putting a dollar value on volunteer hours.
- Professional bios of every member (IFCN 3.4 targets those with "a significant part in its editorial output"; for Civicsmith that is the signers and ratifiers, and pseudonymity rules may apply).
- Publishing audited financial statements or tax returns (newsroom practice, not proportionate for unincorporated groups).
- A fixed $5,000 dollar threshold alone: for a group with a $2,000 budget it hides everything; a share test (5%) plus "any money for this case" is fairer.
