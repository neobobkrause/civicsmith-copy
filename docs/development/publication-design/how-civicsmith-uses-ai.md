# How Civicsmith uses AI

Civicsmith helps groups of residents investigate public matters from public records and publish what they find. Members use AI tools for some of that work. This page explains where AI is used, what it is never allowed to do, how a published case shows where AI was involved, and how to respond to a case.

Rules such as the European Union's AI Act set out how AI-made text published on public matters should be disclosed and reviewed. Civicsmith chooses to follow the direction of those rules where it helps readers. This page is not a claim that Civicsmith or any group meets a particular law, and it is not legal advice.

## Where AI is used

AI is a tool the members use. Members do the investigating, decide what the case says, and publish it.

- **Finding and reading records.** A member can start an AI session to search for records, read documents the group has already collected, and suggest which passages bear on a question. Each session runs within limits the member sets and is recorded under that member's name. Everything it produces is a labelled suggestion. A suggestion becomes part of the group's work only when a member accepts it.
- **Turning documents into text.** When a record is a scan or an image, its text may be produced by character recognition or cleaned up by AI. Each quoted passage keeps a record of how its text was obtained. A machine step never makes that record look more reliable than it is, and a member can check the text against the original and say so.
- **Drafting.** AI can draft:
  - a first version of a case's account;
  - its "what changed" statement for a new edition;
  - help with a field written in the members' own words.

  A draft is always labelled as a draft. It becomes the case's text only when a member takes it. An AI draft of the case's account cannot be published as drafted: a member must rework it. The "what changed" statement may be kept as drafted, and the case records that it was.
- **Weighing the evidence.** How strong a finding is comes from fixed rules applied to its sources and the links between them. It is never an AI's judgment. AI cannot set or change a grade.

## What AI never does

- AI never decides what a finding concludes, accepts its own suggestions, vouches for a record, approves a case, signs it, or publishes it. Only members can.
- AI critique is never counted as review. An AI may point out a gap for a member to consider, but checking the case is the members' job (see below).
- Nothing changes after signing, by AI or by anyone else. A correction is a new edition with its own review and signature.

## Review and ratification

Every published edition is reviewed under the published standard `civicsmith-review/1`, "How a published case is reviewed". In short:

- Members with knowledge of the matter examine the case's substance before it is signed.
- At a minimum, each finding is checked against the public records it cites, and each quoted passage against its source. The case says where a passage was not checked.
- Spelling and formatting checks, automated checks and anything an AI produces do not count as review.
- The group that ratified the case answers for it. An owner of the case signs it, and the signature covers the exact text.

## What the signer affirms

When an owner signs an edition, they also affirm, inside the signed record, that every use of AI in its text is recorded. That includes help from AI tools outside Civicsmith, which the software cannot see and which a member declares.

## How a published case shows where AI was involved

- Where you first meet a published case, it tells you that the case was reviewed and ratified by the group, and that AI was used as a tool.
- Each sentence of the case's own text records where it came from:
  - written by a member;
  - drafted by AI and edited by a member;
  - drafted by AI and kept as drafted;
  - AI help that a member declared.

  This record is part of the signed case, so it cannot be changed without breaking the signature. You can open it on any published case. It is not shown by default.
- The rule is cautious. If a field began as an AI draft, every sentence in it that is not word for word the draft is marked "drafted by AI and edited by a member", even one the member wrote fresh.
- Cases published before this record existed say "not stated". They never claim the text was written by a member.
- The record is machine-readable, and other groups' software can check it alongside the signature.

## How to respond

Every case has a public docket: its signed record of what happened after publication. To respond to a case, use its docket. This includes when:

- you are named in it;
- you hold a record it cites;
- you think it is wrong;
- you think an AI-use record is missing or wrong.

The group files responses on the docket and lists them there as the docket's rules say. When the group corrects a case, the new edition and what changed appear on the same docket.

## Keeping up

Civicsmith follows developments in trust and transparency, starting with the EU AI Act, and adopts them where they serve readers. When the way Civicsmith uses AI changes, this page changes with it. A change to the review standard comes as a new standard with a new id. An edition always names the standard it was reviewed under.
