# How a published case is reviewed

Standard `civicsmith-review/1`

Every case edition published with Civicsmith names this standard in its signed record (`review.standard`). The standard says what review the group carried out before it published, who answers for the case, how AI was involved and how that is shown, and how to report a problem.

Civicsmith adopts this standard voluntarily. It is modelled on the EU Code of Practice on transparency of AI-generated content, Commitment 4 ("Human Review and Editorial Control for Published Text", final 10 June 2026), and on the European Commission's guidelines on Article 50 of the AI Act, C(2026) 7702, paragraphs 134 to 136. It is not a statement that any law applies to Civicsmith or to a group.

## 1. Who answers for a case

- The group named on the case as having ratified it holds editorial responsibility for that edition. The group decided what it says and chose to publish it.
- A member of the group who is an owner of the case signed the edition. The signature covers the exact text of the edition.
- Members who approved the edition before signing are listed with it.
- How Civicsmith uses AI, and how that relates to rules such as the EU AI Act, is explained on the page [How Civicsmith uses AI](../how-civicsmith-uses-ai.md).
- To respond to a publication, use its docket (section 5).

## 2. What review means

Before an edition is signed, members of the group with knowledge of the matter examine its substance. They do more than read it over. At a minimum:

1. **Each finding is checked against the public records it cites.** A finding that its records do not support is corrected or removed.
2. **Each quoted passage is checked against its source.** The edition records any passage the group did not check, and says so where the passage appears.
3. **The case's own account and statements are read for accuracy and fairness** against the findings they rest on.

None of these counts as review:

- spelling, grammar or formatting checks;
- automated checks run by the software;
- any critique, summary or "review" produced by an AI system.

These may help the members. They never stand in for them.

## 3. After signing, nothing changes

- The signature covers the edition's exact text. Anyone can check it with the group's published keys.
- No change can be made to a signed edition, by AI or by anyone else. A correction is a new edition with its own review and signature, and its "what changed" statement is recorded on the case's public docket.
- An edition whose text differs from what was signed fails the signature check. So a reader can confirm that no AI change was made after sign-off.

## 4. How AI was involved, and how that is shown

- Members may use AI tools while building a case, for example to draft a first version of the account or of a statement. A member then reworks the draft. AI tools never make a finding's conclusions.
- Each sentence of the case's own text records where it came from:
  - **written by a member**;
  - **drafted by AI and edited by a member** (any sentence in a field that began as an AI draft and is not word for word the draft);
  - **drafted by AI and kept as drafted** (only where the product allows it: the "what changed" statement);
  - **AI help declared by a member**, for help from a tool outside Civicsmith that the product could not see.
- The signer affirms, in the signed record, that every use of AI in the text is recorded.
- Where a reader first meets the case, it says that the case was reviewed and ratified by the group, with AI used as a tool. The sentence-by-sentence record is available to any reader on request, and is not shown by default.
- Quoted passages from public records also record how their text was obtained, for example a member's transcription or an AI transcription, with the member checks made.

## 5. Responding to a publication

- Each case has a public docket, its signed record of what happened after publication. Anyone can send the group a response to a publication, including a person named in the case, someone who holds a record it cites, or a reader who thinks an AI-use record is missing or wrong. The group files the response on the case's docket and lists it there as the docket's rules say.
- A correction the group makes is a new edition. Its "what changed" statement appears on the same docket.

## 6. Changes to this standard

A change to this standard is published as a new standard with a new id (`civicsmith-review/2`, and so on). Each edition keeps the id it was signed with, so a reader can always find the standard it was reviewed under.
