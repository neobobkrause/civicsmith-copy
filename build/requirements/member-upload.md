# member-upload — requirements

**Status** · Split from `capture` on 5 Nov 2026 (BOB, coordinator standing rule of 5 Nov 2026; the op-grades-folds pattern), to bring `build/requirements/capture.md` under the requirements cap. The requirements below are copied from `build/requirements/capture.md` as it stands on `job/capture` verbatim, each line, sub-bullet and mark unchanged, under the same numbers, so a test that names "R86: ..." stays true. They are a member's file and a received message, as the job proposed (`docs/requirements-notes/capture.md` on `job/capture`), kept under their `capture` numbers rather than renumbered; the evidence store, R112's render allowance and the other rules stay in `capture`, whose terms these requirements use. Their change history is the one kept in `docs/requirements-notes/capture.md`. Until a job of this module moves the code they rule, it lives in `bio-plane/src/capture/`.

## Public

- **R86** A signed-in member brings into the record a file she holds, which no one fetched. Refusals, in order, each writing nothing: the member-session fence: a `by` that is not a signed-in member's stamp (absent, not a string, blank, or a machine identity) is refused `MEMBER_SESSION_REQUIRED` (403), so a machine credential is refused by it; a `statement` (her words on where the file came from, required) that is absent, not a string, blank (white space only) or over 2,000 characters, `UPLOAD_NO_STATEMENT`, a row in this module's table (R37), the next free number of C-118, awaiting promotion's stamp; a `name`, when given, that is not a string or is over 300 characters, the required-argument refusal naming it; zero bytes, `EMPTY`, and more than the capture size limit (`acquisition` R114), `TOO_LARGE` (413) with its size and the limit (R111) and the stream cancelled, each answered as `acquisition` R10 answers it. Otherwise, in one act: the bytes are hashed as they arrive and held under their own digest in parts of 8 MiB (`acquisition` R10's form); one acquisition receipt is written (`provenance.recordReceipt`) with `via: "upload"`, naming the upload as its address (`upload:<sha256>`), with `by`, the instant and her statement; and the answer carries a provenance document built as a pulled knock's document, with these differences: `capture.method` `uploaded`, `capture.actor_class` `member` and `capture.actor` `by` (the upload is attributed to her); `source` `{kind: "uploader", receipt: {sha256, bytes, received}}`, naming no member (`sources` R12: the member is never recorded as the source of what someone else gave her); her statement as `origin_statement: {text, words_of: by, evidence_of_truth: false}`; `name`, when given, as `name_stated`, also her statement, never the stored file's name (`file` is named from the digest); `profile` `acquisition.profileOf` over the bytes, as any capture is profiled, with no response, so no header and no `transport` is recorded or invented (`acquisition` R39's rule); `locator` the upload's address; `retrieved` the receipt's instant. It is graded received, never fetched, exactly as a pulled knock is (K509 (3); `provenance` R51): it earns no fetched letter; its grade is the member's authored letter under the ceiling, stated as authored, never as measured; and its existence at the upload's instant is proven by the receipt's timestamp (chain of custody from her upload's receipt). Bytes the record already holds answer `existed: true` with the held capture's digest and no new document, the receipt being written as a second sighting (`provenance` R15). A later fetch of the same bytes from a public address (`acquire`, any arm) writes its own receipt for the same digest beside the upload's: the upload's receipt and statement stay, nothing is regraded, and the capture's grade is the strongest of its receipts' answers (`provenance` R59's rule; K1852 (3)), so the fetched letter then stands and the capture reads as fetched (`provenance` R62). Like `acquire`, it writes no bundle. It takes `within`, a synchronous function (`store-door` R7): it is called with the document inside the upload's own transaction, after the receipt and the actor are written. An answer `{ok: false, …}` rolls the whole upload back (no receipt, no actor) and is the upload's answer; a throw, or an answer that is not synchronous, rolls it back likewise and answers `UPLOAD_WITHIN_FAILED` (500, a programming fault, no row), its `detail` one fixed sentence, never the thrown message. Any other answer is carried as `within` beside the upload's own. Bytes the record already holds (`existed: true`) do not call it. So the control plane promotes the document at `collected` in the same act (`store-door` R7), and the document is held for review (R77) as any capture. (T51 fix round 2; K4331) Bytes the record's register files under no document but a receipt names (an upload interrupted before it was filed) also answer `existed: true`, and with `document`, built as a new upload's from this upload's statement, name and type; the receipt is written as a second sighting, no actor is recorded and `within` is not called. Bytes the register files answer `existed: true` with no document, as before.

- **R92** (R085-3; K3477; WALK-085 F1; amends R86) `uploadCapture` takes `type`, the media type the member's browser declared for the file (optional; when given, a string of at most 255 characters, else the required-argument refusal naming it). When `type` is a text type (`text/*`, or one `format-registry` names as textual) and the bytes are text (`acquisition` R102's rule: UTF-8, UTF-16 or a declared or detected legacy charset), the bytes are profiled with `type` as their declared content type, so the profile is taken from text (`profiled_from_text: true`), and the profile records the type as the uploader's declaration (`declared_by: "uploader"`), never as a measured transport header; R86's other rules stand (no response, no transport recorded). A text `type` over bytes that are not text, or any other `type`, is recorded as declared and profiles the bytes exactly as R86 does without it. Without `type`, R86 unchanged. Test: a two-line plain-text file uploaded with `text/plain` is profiled from text with its format determined and the declaration recorded as the uploader's. Controls: the same bytes with no `type` profile as today; `text/plain` over bytes `acquisition` R102 does not decode as text is not profiled from text; a `type` over 255 characters is refused writing nothing. (T51 fix round 5, E5; K4541) The rule is `acquisition`'s `textTypeOf` (its R102), the one code the upload and the knock's pull share; this module keeps no copy. (Amended, T51 fix round 6, G41 follow-up; K4678.) For a file held in parts the rule is applied to its first 64 KiB (less a code point cut at their end); see R107.

- **R93** (R085-31; K3528; `walk-085-r5` D2; amends R92) An upload whose declared `type` is empty or absent, and whose file `name` has a text extension (`.txt`, `.md`, `.csv`), is read as text when its bytes are text (`acquisition` R102's rule), as R92 reads a text `type`. Test: a two-line `notes.md` with no `type` is profiled from text; control: the same bytes named `notes.bin`, and a `notes.md` of bytes that are not text, are profiled as R86 does. (T51 fix round 5, E5; K4541) The rule is `acquisition`'s `textTypeOf` (its R102), the one code the upload and the knock's pull share; this module keeps no copy. (Amended, T51 fix round 6, G41 follow-up; K4678.) For a file held in parts the rule is applied to its first 64 KiB (less a code point cut at their end); see R107.

## Later requirements (T51, T53)

- **R103** (fix round 4b, F2, F5; K4487, K4489, K4497; amends R22, R74) **A large upload is kept whole; owed sessions are never pruned.** `uploadCapture` (optional `boundMs`, `keepLater`) past `MULTIPART_KEEP_MAX` keeps the whole through acquisition's `keepPast` (awaited within the bound, else handed to the plane), the answer carrying `keep_pending: true` when handed; the upload is filed in its parts either way. A capture session holds owed resume state until dropped: the clock never removes it; `loadCaptureSession` answers `found: false` for an unknown session and, for one past its expiry, `found: true`, `expired: true` with its state; `dropCaptureSession` alone removes a row (so `loadCaptureSession` is no longer a writer, R74); `liveCaptureSessions` lists only unexpired sessions. Test: a 40 MiB five-part upload through the real `putFromParts`: the whole held; a session past its hour still loads, `expired: true`. (fix round 7, G79; K4736) The parts of a file held in parts are stored from the stream and, up to `MULTIPART_KEEP_MAX`, their buffers are put whole once: no stored part is read back, hashed again or ranged for its head. Past it, when the evidence store offers `putFromParts` and a ledger is at hand (`keepLater`), the keep is handed to the ledger at once and the answer says `keep_pending: true`; with no ledger, or one that declines, it is awaited within the bound as before. Test: `r7-upload-t51.test.mjs`; platform: `r7-upload-plane.test.mjs`.

- **R107** (T51 fix round 6, G41 follow-up; K4670, K4678; amends R92, R93, R101) **A file held in parts is profiled from its declared type or name as one part is.** `uploadCapture` of a file over one 8 MiB part judges, by `acquisition`'s `textTypeOf` (R102), the first 64 KiB of the file (`UPLOAD_HEAD`), less a UTF-8 code point cut at their end; when the head is text, the declared text type, or with none the `.txt`, `.md` or `.csv` name, is the content type `profileOf` is given, so a CSV over one part is profiled `csv`. The first part's byte signature still decides first (a PDF declared `text/csv` is `pdf`), a declaration is recorded as the uploader's and a name is not, and a head that is not text, or a declared type that is not text, leaves the profile as R101 has it. New uploads only. Test: `g41b-t51.test.mjs`; platform: `g41b-plane.test.mjs`. **Amended (fix round 9, G170; K4985):** the upload hands acquisition's profile the file's name and its declared type (acquisition R17), so the format is decided as for a fetched file. Test: control-plane seam §13 through the upload. Control: an upload with no name is profiled from its bytes as before.

- **R108** (fix round 9, G127b, G127c; K4833, K4837; amends R86) **An upload is one capture under its whole digest.** The digest of an upload, however many parts it is held in, is the whole's, hashed as the body arrives: the receipt's address (`upload:<sha256>`), the document's `capture.sha256`, the actor, the reading and every row an upload writes are under that digest, never the first part's, so the document's identity equals the whole's and one reading stands under it and its sentence names the type. A part's digest is no capture and is never registered or looked up (`provenance` R74). The same file uploaded by a second member is refused, if at all, for the whole only (`provenance` R76). Test: SHEET C02 and SCAN E7, a multipart TIFF and JPEG over 8 MiB: the document digest equals the whole's, one reading under it; two members uploading the same bytes share the whole's digest and no part's. Control: a one-part upload is unchanged.

- **R109** (fix round 9, G126, G126a; K4835 (2); 2c item 1; amends R86, R103) **An upload registers its job row in its own transaction.** `uploadCapture` registers the work it owes through the registrar `registerJob`, read as `keepLater` is (the store's `registerJob` when it is a function, else `opts.registerJob`, else the upload goes on without a row and says so in its answer, never silently), and imports nothing from a later module. Inside the synchronous function it gives `within`'s transaction (R86, R74), after the receipt and the actor, it registers one row keyed by the whole sha for each kind it owes: `reading` always; `unpack` when the profile names `zip` (R110); `keep` when R103 hands the keep of a large upload to the plane (that handover is this row). Arming the alarm is a separate step after the commit. A `registerJob` that answers `{ok: false}` rolls the upload back and is answered as a stated refusal, never swallowed. Bytes already held (`existed: true`) register again idempotently, one row. Test, on test-support's `jobRegistrarStub`: a kill between the put and the transaction finds one row and no inline read; an upload answers with its row held; a refusing registrar rolls the upload back with no receipt and no actor; a second upload of the same bytes leaves one row. Control: the same upload with no registrar says so in its answer and is otherwise filed as R86 states. A store with no registrar answers `jobs: {not_registered: [{kind, reason: "NO_REGISTRAR"}]}`; a registrar that refuses or throws rolls the upload back and answers `JOB_NOT_REGISTERED` 502 with `job: {kind, reason}`; the rows are `{kind: "reading", sha, carrier}`, `unpack` for a ZIP profile and `keep` with `parts` when the keep past `MULTIPART_KEEP_MAX` is handed on (K4891). (fix round 9, G152; K4958) No `reading` row is registered for an upload stated over its kind's whole-read ceiling (`boundOf` of its kind: it is never read, so no reading is owed; the statement is the answer, `capture/index.mjs:908`); and a single-part upload hands the bytes it holds to `profileOf` (its `profileBytes`), so the profile never reads the stored object back whole (`acquisition/index.mjs:2070-2073`).

- **R110** (fix round 9, G126f, X3; amends R86) **An uploaded archive is opened as a fetched one is.** An upload whose profile names `zip` (`acquisition` R17) registers an `unpack` job for its whole sha (R109) and is opened by `acquisition.unpack` under the rules a fetched archive meets (its R38, R40, R114): each member it can cut out unambiguously is filed at the archive's grade (an upload's, received and not fetched, `provenance` R63), read separately, and the archive's list shows each member's state, never `waiting` for ever. An archive uploaded twice, or also fetched, opens once. Test: DOCS A1 and A12, an uploaded ZIP of 3 text members: each member filed, read and searchable, and the archive's entries not `waiting`; the same archive uploaded twice leaves one set of members. Control: an upload that is not an archive registers no `unpack` row.

- **R111** (fix round 9, G124b, G124g, G135d; K4831 (3), (4); K4838 (6); amends R21, R86) **A body is bounded before it is held, and the ceilings are stated.** The ceiling on a capture's size (R86's `TOO_LARGE`, R21's put) is `acquisition`'s `CAPTURE_MAX` (its R114, quoted from `READ_BOUNDS`; an archive as uploaded meets the table's archive limit, the standard's 500 MB and today's lower alpha figure active), and the platform's request-body ceiling is the request-body entry of `PLATFORM_FIGURES` (`runtime-limits`; 104,857,600 B including framing as the zone measured, K4838 (6)), each quoted and never written here as a literal. A body over either is refused 413 with its size and the figure (`{size, bound}`), the stream cancelled and nothing written, before it is held whole. `uploadCapture` and `putCapture` hold a body once, not as the request's buffer plus a copy (a put held two or three), counted by the byte ledger (`runtime-limits`). Test: a 24 MiB text upload through the real door stays under the object's cap with the ledger counting; a body of the request ceiling plus 1 is refused naming its size and the figure with no byte held. Control: a body at the ceiling is accepted. The ceiling is the lower of `CAPTURE_MAX` and `PLATFORM_FIGURES.request_body_max` (104,857,600 B, the platform's measured body cap, K4838 (6)); a `declared` length is judged before a byte is read; both ops take a byte `ledger` and release it on every path (K4891). A one-request upload refused for the request-body ceiling that `CAPTURE_MAX` still admits answers `in_parts: true`; a file sent in parts (R113) is bound by `CAPTURE_MAX` alone, judged on the size declared at its begin before any part is accepted.

- **R113** (fix round 9; G127f; K4838 (6), K4891; amends R86, R111) **A file past the request-body ceiling is sent in parts through the Worker and written with R2's own multipart upload.** `beginPartedUpload({sha256, bytes, statement, name, type, by})` (`op=captureuploadbegin`), `putUploadPart({upload, index, bytes, sha256, by, declared, ledger})` (`op=captureuploadpart`), `completePartedUpload({upload, parts, by, within, at})` (`op=captureuploadcomplete`) and `abortPartedUpload({upload, by})` (`op=captureuploadabort`). The begin is refused as R86 refuses an upload before a byte is read and judges the declared size against `CAPTURE_MAX` alone (`TOO_LARGE` 413 with `size` and `bound`), then answers the part size and the number of parts. Parts go in order, each exactly the part size (the last the remainder) and checked against its own digest as it arrives; a part put twice is stored once; a completion with a part missing or out of order is refused and writes nothing. The digest of all the bytes, hashed in order across the parts, is the one declared at the begin or the upload is aborted and nothing is held (never a part's digest, R108). The completion makes one object under the whole's digest and files it as R86, R108-R110 state (one receipt, the actor, one job row through the registrar in the filing transaction, the same answer); the document names no parts. The byte ledger holds at most one part. (G173; K5001) An upload survives the object being taken out of memory between two parts (the platform does so after about 10 s with no event): the digest of the parts so far is kept with the upload's row, or taken again from the parts held, so the next part in order is accepted whatever the pause; `UPLOAD_LOST` is answered only when the parts held are themselves gone. An upload not completed is aborted at its owner's word or 24 hours after its begin and holds nothing; the object's session state is a row without a byte of the file. Only the member who began an upload may use it. **Amended (fix round 9, G176; K5005):** a part answered without its bytes being kept (a part already held, put again; a refusal after the request is read) is answered only after its body is read and discarded, never more than the part size, so a client still sending sees the answer, never a closed connection. Test: the re-put of a held 16 MiB part and a late part after an abort, each sent whole, read their JSON answers 20 of 20 under four copies at once. (Fix round 10, cause 29; K5056) The four parted operations (`captureuploadbegin`, `captureuploadpart`, `captureuploadcomplete`, `captureuploadabort`) admit the same classes as `captureupload`, the founder's among them, and the receipt names the founder's stamp; a session without `contribute` is still refused. Test: the founder's session begins a parted upload, puts two parts and completes it, and aborts another, each answered 200; control: a session without `contribute` is refused.

- **R114** (fix round 9, G180; K5020; amends R113) **The running digest of a parted upload is record-grammar's.** The SHA-256 kept with a parted upload's row and taken up again after the object leaves memory is `record-grammar`'s `createResumableSha256` (its R64; its `snapshot()` is what the row keeps); the module carries no SHA-256 of its own (`sha256-resume.mjs` removed).
