## Public

### Purpose

The plan screen, with its own `SCREENS` and the `screen.plan.*` words. It uses `screens-action` (`plan.mjs` imports `readOnly`, `WRITE_HELP`, `actName`, `citeOf` and `later` from its `send-steps.mjs`).

### Provides

- **R1** (moved from screens-action:R1) Reads the plan (`plan`: its subjects, called matters in member words, each with its determination and escalation stage; its options with category, regulated end date, subject and disposition; its scenarios and their phases; what each started option became) and its checks (`planproposals`, the assistant's suggestions, drawn only while the assistant is reachable, labelled machine work "not yet in the plan"). The checks strip states each check, such as a scenario with no branch for a hostile response. Options sort by category, regulated end date, matter or disposition, and are picked as rows (`interface-shell` R19). Acts: `plansubjectadd` "Add a matter"; `optionadd`; `optionpropose` "Ask the assistant for options" (reachable only); `optionadopt` (a suggestion, by the member's own act); `optiondispose` "Choose" or "Decline" (with a reason where declining); `scenarioset` "Lay out a scenario"; `checkpointrecord` "Judge" a checkpoint; `optionstartpreview` "Start…" (opening `start-send` for the option); `itemtakeup` "Take this up" on an item nobody has taken up (routing its to-dos to the member; nobody assigns anybody); `planclose` "Close plan…" with a reason.
- **R2** (moved from screens-action:R13) (T48; N1043; K3149, K3207; `action-plans` R51; `interface-shell` R55; amends R1) On `plan`, "Ask the assistant for options" (the registry's label `act.optionpropose.label.plan` and help `act.optionpropose.does`) sends `planask` with the plan, only while the assistant is reachable (`interface-shell` R20); its answer's run is drawn running (re-read by `cs.later`, as `screens-inquiry` R8), and the run's proposals reach the tray (`planproposals`, labelled machine work). `existed: true` draws the run already asked. `optionpropose` is not drawn for this act. Refusals by their words (`AI_*`, `PLAN_CLOSED`). Test: the act sends `planask` and the tray is re-read when the run ends; control: no `optionpropose` is sent.
- **R3** (moved from screens-action:R15) (T49; N1090; DEC-236 (B203); amends R13) On `plan`, "Ask the assistant for options" is explained by `act.planask.does`, never `act.optionpropose.does`. Test: the help drawn is `act.planask.does`'s.
- **R4** (moved from screens-action:R18) (T49; D5 **B**, its half; D20 **M**; `ai-runs` R81; amends R13, R1) On `plan`, four rules:
  - **The run.** `planask`'s refusal is drawn by its words. "The assistant is looking for options" is drawn only while its run is running, as `airun` answers it. An answer of `ok: true` that carries `started: false`, `dispatched: false` or `withheld` is drawn as its refusal (rule 6).
  - **Titles.** The plan names its project and question by their titles, each a reference, never `INQ-…` or `inquiry:INQ-…`.
  - **References.** A reference is picked from a list the record answers (`cs.choice` over record data), never typed.
  - **Categories.** These are drawn by their words, never as lowercase codes.

  Test: each over a fixture. Control: a running plan run draws the sentence, and an ended one does not.
- **R5** (moved from screens-action:R25) (T51-122; DEC-329 (6); N1237; K3772, K3774; `action-plans` R6, R7, R40; amends R1) **`plan` opened on a project draws one plan, the project's plans, or where one is started.** The screen's subject may be a plan or a project (the form of the id tells which: a project's id begins `PROJ-`, a plan's ends `-plan`; `interface-shell` R9 routes `/app/plan/<subject>` with either). On a project it reads `plans` (`action-plans` R7: `project`, rows `{id, project, title, state, opened_by, opened_at}` and the `subjects`, `closing_note` and `out_of_view` the code also answers and this screen does not draw, at most 200 a page, `truncated` and `cursor`, paged by `after`, the viewer's sight applied; `action-plans` R7 states none of the row's fields, gap (2)) and draws the drawing's three states: **one live plan** (exactly one plan whose state is `open` or `paused`) opens that plan at once, R1's page, with no list; **several live plans, or only closed ones**: the project's plans (the heading `screen.plan.list.title` with the project as its sub, the section `screen.plan.list` and its help `.list.help`), live ones first (`open`, `paused`) and newest first by `opened_at`, then closed, each row the plan's title as a reference opening it, its state (`screen.plan.state.open`, `.paused`, `.closed`) and `screen.plan.opened` (`{date}`, `{name}` the opener's handle, the founder by `member.founder.unnamed`); **none**: `screen.plan.none`, which says where a plan is started (under the project's questions, "Plan what to do", or on one question's own page), with no list and no empty heading (R14). A plan the viewer may not see is not listed; a read that fails is drawn failed, never as "no plan". `truncated` is said. A guide step that needs a project for `plan` (`screens-wizards` R17, T51-115) opens this screen on it. Test: a project with one open plan opens it; with an open and a paused plan lists both, open first, newest first; with only a closed plan lists it; with none draws `screen.plan.none`. Control: a plan id as the subject opens that plan as before; a failed `plans` read is drawn failed.
- **R6** (moved from screens-action:R44) (C73; DEC-376; amends R1) **The plan's forms carry what the plane takes.** "Add an option" carries who it is directed to, the dates that bind it each with its basis, the tier (legal options only), the lobbying mark with the requirement it seeks; "Lay out a scenario" carries each phase's branches and order (when it starts, after which phase, where it goes if its checkpoint is met or not); an option can be marked done, blocked or returned to undecided (as it can be chosen or declined), each with a reason and a reminder where the design draws one. The bodies carry the names `action-plans` declares. Test: each form sends the declared names; a done, blocked or undecided mark sends its reason. Control: a refusal is drawn by its words. (fix round 4, K4473) C53: a reminder day with the date left blank takes the option's first date (one with none takes none).
- **R7** (moved from screens-action:R53) (E1; DEC-384, U417; K4197; amends R1) **The plan's unnamed parts are named by place.** An option with no summary is drawn as `screen.plan.option.unnamed` and a phase with no name as `screen.plan.phase.unnamed` (by its place in the list), never by its id; the Judge control is drawn only when the checkpoint is due, otherwise `screen.plan.judge.notdue`; a checkpoint's miss is drawn as "met" or "not met" by key, never as the raw value. Test: an option and a phase without names draw the unnamed keys and no id; a checkpoint not due draws the notdue key and no Judge. Control: a due one draws Judge; `{miss}` draws both words by key.
- **R8** (moved from screens-action:R67) (T53; JOURNEYS; changes the plan's "Take this up"; owner `plan-work` R5, R11; answers R-113) **A taken item stays taken.** After "Take this up" a reload shows it taken (by whom, her target date) and does not offer it again; whether the queue shows a to-do for it is for the job to settle against `plan-work` and `queue-producers` (the register did not trace it). *(not yet met: T53)* Test: take up then reload: the item shows as taken; control: another member still sees it open.
- **R9** (moved from screens-action:R68) (T53; JOURNEYS; changes R2 and plan options table (R53 of the module); answers R-125) **The options table says where each goes and its tier.** Each option row draws the office it goes to and its tier; "Assumed: not shown yet ..." is explained in a sentence. Words owed. Test: an option with an office and a tier draws both; control: an option with none draws "no office chosen".
- **R10** (moved from screens-action:R69) (T53; JOURNEYS; changes R51 of the module; answers R-112) **A checkpoint draws its test and where it leads.** A checkpoint draws the test it applies and "if met, go to ... / if not met, go to ...", as well as its judge day. Test: a checkpoint "Did the officer answer by the date?" draws its test and both branches; control: a checkpoint with no branches draws "no next step set".
