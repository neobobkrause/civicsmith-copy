## Public

- **R38** Opens an archive the record holds and files each file it can cut out unambiguously. Refusals, each writing nothing: `BAD_SHA` for an `archiveSha` not 64 hex; `UNPACK_NOT_PERMITTED` for a caller R40 does not admit; `ARCHIVE_NOT_HELD` when no acquisition receipt or register row names the digest (`provenance.registerHolds`, its R5), or its bytes are not held whole or in every part with a stored checksum that agrees (`provenance.partsHeld`, its R7); `ARCHIVE_UNREADABLE` with `ooxml.listArchive`'s `why` when its listing answers one of `ooxml` R3's reasons, `source_unreadable` or `ARCHIVE_ENTRIES_MAX` (with `limit`); `ARCHIVE_AMBIGUOUS` with the listing's `detail`; `ARCHIVE_TOTAL_MAX` (with `limit`) when the listing's verdict is that limit, the archive listed and nothing cut (`ooxml` R27, R28). Otherwise it reads the listing in index order from the first entry with no recorded outcome, within R40's budgets, and records one outcome for each entry it reaches. (ACQUISITION #13 J1; K2100) A held capture that is not an archive (an office or OpenDocument file among them) is refused `NOT_AN_ARCHIVE` exactly as R41 refuses it, after `ARCHIVE_NOT_HELD` and before any listing; nothing is filed. (T50; N1064; amends R38) `MEMBER_MAX` (C-139.9) equals `ARCHIVE_TOTAL_MAX` in `ooxml`, so one member declared over it always puts the archive over the total, and the whole archive is refused `ARCHIVE_TOTAL_MAX` first (the member's own verdict is not reachable through `unpack`). Test: a test pins that `MEMBER_MAX` equals `ARCHIVE_TOTAL_MAX` (a change to either figure fails it and asks for a fixture over `MEMBER_MAX`), and a listing declaring one member `MEMBER_MAX + 1` bytes answers `ARCHIVE_TOTAL_MAX` with its `limit` for every entry, C-139.9's row being proved by its code in the table.
  - a `directory` or `symlink` verdict is listed, never cut, and a symlink's target is stated, never followed;
  - any other verdict but `ok` (`MEMBER_ENCRYPTED`, `MEMBER_METHOD_UNSUPPORTED`, `MEMBER_AMBIGUOUS`, `MEMBER_MAX`, `ARCHIVE_RATIO_MAX`) is not filed, under that name;
  - an `ok` entry is cut with `ooxml.streamMember`, its bytes hashed and stored under their own digest in parts of 8 MiB, each part under its own SHA-256 with its checksum (R10's form), and counts as cut only once `done` answers `ok: true`; a refusal from `done` (`MEMBER_CORRUPT` with its `detail`, or `source_unreadable`) is not filed, under that name, and no byte stored for it is ever named as the file;
  - a cut file whose digest the register already holds under an existing bundle, or that an earlier entry filed, is not filed again (`provenance` R2's one home): its `unpacked` receipt is written as a second sighting (`provenance` R15) and the entry is `already_held`, with the digest;
  - every other cut file gets its `unpacked` receipt (`provenance` R15) and its document (R39), and is `filed`.
  Nothing is filed at a lower letter, and nothing is filed but what the listing and the cut agree on (K1852 (1)). The answer carries `archive` (`{sha256, entries, declared_total}`), `documents` (R39's, one per file filed by this call, for the caller to promote), `already_held` (`[{index, sha256}]`), `not_filed` (`[{index, reason, code, check, translation}]`, `limit` with a limit's name), `nested` (the digests of files filed by this call that are archives themselves, R40), `waiting` (the count of entries with no outcome yet) and `complete` (true when every entry has one). Calling `unpack` again for the same archive resumes from the first entry with no outcome; an entry with one is never cut again.
  (T53; N824, R136: for each entry it reaches, R38 also registers the entry's `member` job row, idempotently, in the same synchronous function that records the entry's outcome, and files through `fileMember`; its refusals, outcomes and every answer are unchanged.)

- **R39** Each file R38 files answers one provenance document, built as R16's with these differences: `capture.method` `unpacked`; `capture.grade` the letter `provenance.captureGrade` answers for the file once its receipt is written (its archive's, `provenance` R59), absent when that answer has none, and the archive document's `grade_basis` then stated; `capture.actor_class` and `actor` by R16's rule, for R38's caller; `capture.sha256`, `bytes`, `encoding: "binary"` and `content_type` from R17's profile of the file's own bytes (no response exists, so no header and no `transport` is recorded or invented); `container` as `provenance` R42 states it (`archive_sha256`, `index`, `path` the decoded name or null, `name_raw`, `method`, `crc32`, `compressed`, `uncompressed`, `local_offset`, `member_sha256`, `dos_time_stated`), with `name_shared` and `path_unsafe` as the listing answers them; `provenance_chain` one hop, `who` by `firstHopWho` (R33), asserting that these bytes are entry `<index>` (`<path>`, the archive's own claim) of the archive `<archiveSha>` held by your group's Civicsmith, cut out and verified by size and CRC-32 at `<retrieved>`, `bound: false`, `via: "unpacked"`, citing the archive's digest and never copying its hop or receipt; `origin` the archive document's; `attestation_attempts: []` (R20); `parts` when stored in more than one part; `locator` the archive's document address followed by `#zip:<index>`; `retrieved` the instant of the cut (this instance's clock, to the second); `file` `snapshots/<name>`, the name made from the archive's digest and the index, never from the entry's name; and `profile` R17's over the file's bytes. A stated time is recorded as the archive's statement with no zone, never as a fact. The caller promotes each document at `collected` as an Information document of its own in the archive's project, held for review beside its archive (Intake §4; `capture` R77 groups them). **Amended (fix round 9, G161, G163; K4980):** a member cut from an archive with no letter carries the ARCHIVE's `grade_basis` (provenance C-18.1: the basis of the archive whose route decided it), never its own `CAPTURE_UNPACKED_FROM_ARCHIVE`; and its type, when its bytes carry no magic, is R102's `textTypeOf` of its bytes and its entry name, so a `.txt`, `.md` or `.csv` member is read as text. Test: an uploaded ZIP of three text members passes C-18.1 and each is read (control-plane seam §6 A1, A12); control: a fetched letter-graded archive's members carry its letter as before. Test: `r123-batch-d.test.mjs`.

- **R40** (K1852 (2); F7; K1881) **When an archive opens, and how far.**
  - An `acquire` (R1–R23) whose document's profile names `zip` (R17), other than a continuation (R12), runs R38 for its capture in the same call, `by` its caller, and answers R38's answer as `unpack` beside `document`; a refusal of R38 is stated there and never fails the capture. The caller promotes the archive's document, then R38's documents.
  - One call cuts at most `UNPACK_CALL_ENTRIES` (100) entries and `UNPACK_CALL_BYTES` (67,108,864: 64 MiB declared uncompressed) of files; the rest wait for the next call.
  - The whole unpack tree, counted from the outermost archive (a file that is itself an archive counting towards the archive it was cut from, and so on up), is held to `ooxml`'s `ARCHIVE_TREE_TOTAL_MAX` and `ARCHIVE_TREE_ENTRIES_MAX` (its R30). An archive opens only at a depth of at most `ARCHIVE_DEPTH_MAX`, the outermost at depth 1; a deeper one is filed as a file and not opened, its listing state naming that limit (R41).
  - Automatic opening is held to `UNPACK_DAILY_BYTES` (1,073,741,824: 1 GiB declared uncompressed) and `UNPACK_DAILY_ENTRIES` (40,000 entries) in each UTC day, over every archive of the group.
  - Past a tree limit or the daily budget, the automatic run stops: the archive stays listed, its remaining entries `waiting` on that name (R41), and waits for a member. A member's own `op=unpack` (a member session, `by` an active member) goes past the tree limits and the daily budget, and counts towards the day, but never past `ooxml`'s per-archive limits or `ARCHIVE_DEPTH_MAX`. The daemon continues an automatic run within every budget. Any other caller is refused `UNPACK_NOT_PERMITTED`.
  - Each figure is exported by its name, and every wait or refusal on one names it and carries its figure as `limit`.

- **R41** `archiveList` answers an archive's entries as R38 recorded them. `archive` is `{sha256, bundle, grade, bytes, entries, declared_total, opened, refused}`: `grade` as `provenance.captureGrade` answers it, `opened` false for an archive held but never opened (each `ok` entry then `waiting`), `refused` R38's whole-archive refusal (`ARCHIVE_AMBIGUOUS`, `ARCHIVE_UNREADABLE`, `ARCHIVE_TOTAL_MAX`) with its row, or null. `summary` counts `filed`, `already_held`, `not_filed`, `folders` and `waiting`. Each entry is `{index, name, name_raw, name_encoding, name_shared, path_unsafe, kind, dos_time_stated, uncompressed, state}`, its names, folders and times exactly as the listing states them, `state` one of: `filed` (with `sha256`, and the `bundle` it was promoted into, held for review beside the archive); `already_held` (with `sha256`: the same bytes the record held already, kept as a second sighting); `folder`; `link` (a symlink, its target stated); `not_filed` (with `code`, `check` and `translation`, R29's row in member words, and `limit` for a limit; an archive refused whole answers its refusal for every entry the listing read); or `waiting` (no outcome yet, with the limit or budget it waits on, or none when it waits only for the next call). `state` filters to one state (`not_filed` is the screen's "only those not filed"). Entries come in index order, at most `limit` (default 200, clamped 1…1000), with `truncated` and `next`, passed back as `after`. Refusals: `BAD_SHA`; `ARCHIVE_NOT_HELD` both for an archive not held and for one whose home the viewer may not see (membership R43), the same answer for each; (N720; K1955) `NOT_AN_ARCHIVE`, with its row (R29), for a capture held and visible to the viewer that R17's rule does not profile as `zip` (an office or OpenDocument file included), so a plain file is never answered as an archive whose listing is refused whole. An entry's `bundle` the viewer may not see is answered null. It writes nothing. `memberOf(captureSha)` answers every archive the capture was filed from or found in, from R38's records, `[]` for none; it writes nothing and never throws. (T50; N1126; SCREENS-INTAKE #4 J3; K3376; amends R41) An `already_held` entry also carries `held_at` and `held_name`, those of the earlier holding of the same bytes: `held_at` the instant the record first held them (`provenance.capturesOf`'s `held_at` for that digest, provenance R12, in the document that holds it) and `held_name` that document's title. Both are null when the earlier holding is no document the viewer may see (as `bundle` is answered null) and when it has no document yet; so `screen.archive.alreadyheld` (`{name}`, `{date}`) is drawn whole only when both are given, and `screen.archive.sum.held` says the plain count otherwise. An entry in any other state carries neither key. Test: an archive with a file whose bytes a visible earlier capture holds answers that entry with `held_at` equal to the capture's `held_at` and `held_name` its title. Control: a viewer who may not see the earlier capture gets `bundle`, `held_at` and `held_name` all null; a `filed` entry carries none of the three keys.

A capture already opened costs none. - **R113** (fix round 9, G127d; K4833; 2c item 3; K4886) **`readRange(store, sha, offset, length) → Promise<Uint8Array | {ok: false, reason}>`: a capture's bytes by its whole digest.** `store` is the record's store, taken first as by every act of this module (K4886). `sha` is the whole's digest only (64 lowercase hex; a `sha:` prefix and case ignored); `offset` and `length` are whole numbers at least 0. The bytes are those of the whole whether the capture is held whole (read from the whole object) or only in parts (read across the parts `provenance.partsOf(sha)` names, its R78), so a range across a part seam equals the same bytes of the whole; a range reaching past the end answers the bytes that exist, and one starting at or past the end an empty array. A whole the record does not hold, a digest that is not 64 hex, or an `offset` or `length` that is not a whole number answers `{ok: false, reason}` and reads nothing; a store failure answers `{ok: false, reason}` with the store's own reason, never a throw. A caller never learns whether the capture is held in parts, and no other module resolves parts. Test: a 100 MB capture in parts (the lazy ZIP writer's, not materialised): `readRange(store, sha, 8 MiB - 10, 20)` equals bytes 8 MiB - 10 to 8 MiB + 10 of the whole; the same bytes held whole answer the same; a range past the end, a malformed digest, a whole not held and a store that fails each answer as stated. Control: a capture held whole under 8 MiB answers its head and tail exactly.

- **R125** (T53; N1259; K4403 (2), (5); K1485; amends R10, R16, R26) **`streamRegister` fetches a register piece, reduces it as it arrives, and stores only the reduced copy as the capture.** `streamRegister(store, {definition, piece, filter, import, resume?, budget_ms, budget_bytes, viewer})` fetches the piece (a whole file, or a zip member read by range through `register-readers` R2) through the host governor and R9's agent, runs `createReducer` (`register-readers` R9) over the decompressed stream, and stores the reduced bytes in parts of 8 MiB, each under its own SHA-256 with its checksum, hashed as written (R10); the original is never stored, in whole or in part, nor kept beyond two windows. - **R126** (T53; N1259; K4403 (2); K4837; amends R10, R114) **A register piece's input is bounded by its own ceiling, its stored output by pieces.** The input a register piece may stream is bounded by `REGISTER_STREAM_MAX`, quoted from `runtime-limits` (set from M-R7; the measured sources reach 5.46 GiB per federal zip and 1.49 GiB for the state's), not by `CAPTURE_MAX` or `READ_BOUNDS` (K5530 F4); over it the call refuses `TOO_LARGE` (413) with `{size, bound, key}` and stores nothing. Test: a generated 6 GiB stream is refused over a 5 GiB bound with size and bound stated and accepted under 8 GiB; 3 segment-sizes of output are 3 captures, the last `final`; a no-match piece stores the header capture. - **R127** (T53; N1259; K4403 (2)) **A piece is read in bounded slices and resumes after any stop without a repeated or lost row.** `streamRegister` stops when `budget_ms` or `budget_bytes` is spent, at a row boundary, and answers `{done, resume?, progress: {bytes_in, rows_in, rows_out, segments_stored}, checkpoint: "offset" | "member_start"}`. `resume` is an opaque token holding the piece, the source offset or member start the next call continues from, the reducer's carry (the unfinished row), the original's hash snapshot (`record-grammar` R64) and the stored segments so far; the next call with it continues. A source that can be read from an offset (stored members, whole files with Range) checkpoints at `offset`; a deflate member checkpoints at `member_start` unless M-R7 shows a decoder state can be kept, and the answer says which. Work repeated after a restart is at most the segment in progress. A segment stored twice by two overlapping calls is stored once (content-addressed), and the second call answers `existed`. Test: a run killed after each of the first 5 slices and resumed ends with the segment digests, counts and original fingerprint of an uninterrupted run; a deflate member answers `member_start`; two calls from one token store each segment once. Control: an uninterrupted run answers no `resume`.

- **R128** (T53; N1259; K4403 (2); K1492 (4); amends R9) **`probeRegister` states a register's size and change without fetching its body.** `probeRegister(store, {definition, piece, filter?, budget_ms})` makes only governed requests: a HEAD or a one-byte range request for `Content-Length`, `ETag`, `Last-Modified` and the range support, and, where the definition's `count` (jurisdictions R129) supports every criterion of the filter, the source's own count query for that filter. It answers `{bytes: {value, basis}, rows: {value, basis}, validators, source_date, reached: [...], timed_out}` with `basis` `stated` (the source's own figure), `counted` (the count query answered for this filter) or `not_stated`; it never calls a row count counted unless the source counted it for that filter, never fetches a body, and stores and records nothing but the governor's pacing. A request that passes `budget_ms` ends the probe with `timed_out: true` and what was reached. Test: a source giving no `Content-Length` gives bytes `not_stated`; a SoQL-style count for a filter of supported criteria gives rows `counted`, with an unsupported one (a name) `not_stated`; a slow count ends at `budget_ms` with `timed_out`; the fake sees no body request. Control: a probe of an unchanged source twice gives equal validators.

- **R129** (T53; N1259; K4403 (7); K1492 (4)) **An unchanged source is not fetched again.** `sourceChanged(probe, lastProbe)` is false when the definition's `refresh.validators` all agree between the two probes (same `ETag`, `Last-Modified`, `Content-Length`, count) and true when any differs or when the definition lists none; `acquisition` refuses a refresh fetch (`REFRESH_UNCHANGED`, with the validators) when it is false, unless the caller passes `force` (a member's act). - **R132** (T53; N1259; K4403 (2); K1492 (1), (4)) **A register is fetched only from its own definition's hosts, under its stated terms.** `streamRegister` and `probeRegister` send a request only to a host among the definition's `system` hosts (a redirect is followed by hand, as R23 does (`host-governor` R16 follows them itself), and only to a host among those hosts, which for FEC's zips includes their storage host, M-R1 row 4; else `HOST_NOT_IN_DEFINITION`), through `host-governor` (admitted or `HOST_COOLING_OFF`) and R9's agent with purpose `register`. The definition's `terms_basis` is recorded on the capture's hop. A team's own definition (`registers` R7) is held to the same rules; a login-gated site or a paid people-search is refused before any request (`jurisdictions` R128; K1492 (2), (3)).
