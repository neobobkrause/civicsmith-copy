## Public

### Purpose

How a group gets its own Civicsmith: the installer Worker's routes, the install, the update and removal into the group's own Cloudflare account, its custody of credentials and the embed step. Its pages and words are `installer-pages`'s, and what it does to the account and the fleet is `installer-steps`'.

### Provides

Terms. The **slug** is the copy's name, and becomes its worker name, its address and its recorded group (instance-setup R2). A **member** is a fleet worker the release's signed fleet statement names. A **step** is one emitted line on the progress page, which ends ok or not.

**The routes: `GET /`, `GET /update`, `POST /begin`, `GET /callback`**
- **R1** [met] `GET /` serves the install page and `GET /update` the update page. Any other path is answered 404 with a plain page. No route stores anything.
- **R2** [not yet met] `POST /begin` takes `{mode, slug, instanceAi?}`. `mode` is `update`, or else `install`. A slug outside `GROUP_SLUG_RE`, or equal to `newgroup`, is refused 400 with the grammar in words. An `instanceAi` that is present but not 16–512 printable characters without spaces is refused 400 by name. Otherwise it answers `{ok: true, authorize}`: the authorize address with a PKCE S256 challenge, a fresh `state`, exactly the five scopes (`workers-scripts.write`, `workers-r2.write`, `account-settings.read`, the Containers write scope, under the name Cloudflare's scope list gives it, M-Q8, and `connectivity-directory.bind` for R45's binding, K2155) and exactly the registered redirect. No field of `/begin` carries a Claude credential (R36). It sets an `HttpOnly; Secure; SameSite=Lax` cookie for 15 minutes, holding the verifier, the state, the slug, the mode, the time and any `instanceAi`. (T51-128; N1151, K3818) **A sixth scope, once it is registered:** `account-logs.read`, the scope that reads Logpush jobs (R46), is asked as the sixth, and "exactly the five" reads "exactly the scopes registered on the OAuth client": it is added to `CFG.SCOPES` only after it is registered there (`CFG.SCOPES` is exactly the scopes registered on the client, nothing more; an unregistered one would fail on Cloudflare's side before the founder returns, where no message of this installer can say so), the registration being Bob's act (K3815: the session's token cannot change the client). Until then the five stand and R46 reads the list without it. Test: `/begin`'s authorize address carries the registered set exactly; control: it carries no scope the client does not hold.
- **R3** [met] `GET /callback` always clears the cookie. An `error` answers "Permission was not granted" and creates nothing. A missing code, a missing or unreadable cookie, a state mismatch, or a cookie older than 15 minutes answers "could not be verified" and creates nothing. Otherwise it streams the progress page and runs the install or the update.

**The install** (steps `auth`, `acct`, `fresh`, `plan`, `r2`, `rel`, `gen`, `install`, `fleet`, `bind`, `ai`, `addr`, `verify`)
- **R4** [met] A refusal at any step before `install` says that nothing was created, and nothing was: the plan probe script is deleted or, when it cannot be, named. The first account the permission lists is used, and it is named on the page.
- **R5** [met] `fresh`: an existing script named for the slug is refused, and nothing is changed. A lookup that fails is refused.
- **R6** [met] `plan`: the plan is found by upload, never by reading a field. A probe upload carrying `limits.cpu_ms` that is refused with code 100328 means Free, and the install is refused, saying that Workers Paid is needed and how to enable it. Any other failure means unknown, and the install is refused. Paid is confirmed.
- **R7** [met] `r2`: both evidence buckets exist afterwards ("already exists" counts), or the install is refused, saying that a payment method is needed.
- **R8** [met] `rel`: the repository's release is installed only when it is newer than the built-in one, its bytes hash to its manifest's `sha256`, and (with signers armed) its `sig` verifies in the release namespace against `ARMED_SIGNERS`. Otherwise the built-in release installs, and the page says why: integrity failed, unsigned, signed by an untrusted key, or unreachable. Nothing that failed verification is ever installed.
- **R9** [met] `gen`: five fresh 32-byte random credentials (the one-time password `ADMIN_TOKEN`, `MEMBER_TOKEN`, `PROBE_TOKEN`, `DAEMON_TOKEN`, and `ACCOUNT_SEAL_SECRET`, the key `credentials` seals members' account references with; K1541, K1678). `INSTANCE_AI_TOKEN` is bound only when the operator supplied a valid one; the installer never generates it. (T36) `gen`: four fresh 32-byte random credentials (the one-time password `ADMIN_TOKEN`, `PROBE_TOKEN`, `DAEMON_TOKEN`, and `ACCOUNT_SEAL_SECRET`); no `MEMBER_TOKEN` is generated (the shared member key is retired; members sign in with their own sessions). R10's "the five credentials" reads "the four credentials".
- **R10** [met] `install`: the plane is uploaded with its `Store` Durable Object (SQLite, migration `v1`), `VERSION`, `INSTANCE_NAME` (the slug), the five credentials, `INSTANCE_AI_TOKEN` when supplied, both buckets, a `SELF` service binding to the slug, `BROWSER`, the members the account already holds, the release's limits (R20) and the plane's compatibility date and flags. When the upload is refused, it retries once without `SELF`, and on success says that the re-check part was left out and the updater adds it. Otherwise it is refused, and nothing is left behind.
- **R12** [met] `bind`: three acts, in this order. First, the plane is bound to the members already present (R10). Second, the members are installed (R11). Third, only when a member was added, the plane is re-uploaded in the update's shape, bound to every member now present. A refusal of that re-upload is named, and the members stay installed.
- **R13** [not yet met] Each member that reads captured bytes is bound to the instance's `CAPTURES` bucket.
- **R14** [met] `addr`: the account's workers.dev prefix is used. When the account has none, one is registered from the slug (or the slug with a short suffix) and the page says so. The copy's address is then enabled. If this step fails, the page says the copy is installed without an address, and that the address can be fixed without starting over.
- **R15** [met] `verify`: `op=selftest` with the probe credential, up to ten tries. Then, when the release's plane can report builds (its source carries `storeVersion` and `memberVersions`), `op=bootstrap&members=1` is asked until every part answers the release. Each part that does not is named: the address, the record store, and each member that is serving another build, misnamed, silent, installed but unbound, installed but unknown, or failed to upload. A container member R38 leaves out (its conditions unmet, its image not pullable) is not a failed upload: it is named at the fleet step with why and what the copy lacks, never a lag here, so the install never fails over it (R38) (T50, K3793). A release that cannot report builds is said to be undetermined for those parts. No success is claimed while a part lags. (T35; F1, K1874) Every request of this step carries the probe credential only in its `Authorization: Bearer <credential>` header (`admission` R20), never in its address or query, so the credential is in no address the installer makes, logs or shows; a test captures each verify request and finds the sentinel credential in its header and in no URL.

**The update**

The copy's version before the update is read (`op=bootstrap`). The upload keeps the credentials and the Durable Object (`keep_bindings`), keeps the buckets unless it re-binds them, and restates `VERSION`, `INSTANCE_NAME`, `SELF`, the members present, `BROWSER`, a fresh `DAEMON_TOKEN`, and `INSTANCE_AI_TOKEN` only when supplied; it never restates a held `ACCOUNT_SEAL_SECRET` (a new one would orphan every sealed reference), binds a fresh one only when the script's settings list none; when the settings cannot be read the whole update is refused before anything is uploaded, naming why (K1678; since fix round 7, `installer-steps` R42, K4758: the held room's place cannot be restated blind). A refused upload leaves the copy as it was, and the page says so. The fleet, bind and verify steps run as in R11, R12 and R15. An update that moved nothing (same version before) says so, and never says "Updated". The update asks R2's scopes, so a group re-consents to the Containers write scope on its first update after T33; an update whose permission lacks it installs everything else and names the container member left out (R38). (T36) The update writes no `MEMBER_TOKEN` and no `ASSISTANT_ENABLED`, and leaves neither behind: one held before the update is deleted, and a refused delete is named, as R36 deletes `INSTANCE_CLAUDE_TOKEN`.
- **R18** [met] When the release is at or after the first release that records a group (0.71.0), and the copy ran an earlier one, the update tells the operator the seed act (`op=instancegroupseed`, for the record and for scratch). When the version before is unknown, the telling is conditional and says why. The installer never performs the act. (T51-128; K3868, DEC-336 (1), K3878; replaces this line's last sentence and DEC-331 (2)'s sending the administrator to a screen) **The installer performs it, R78:** it records the team's short name itself, the act is no longer the operator's, and the telling this line describes is drawn only for a copy the run could not record (R78), `installer.team.unrecorded.how` reading "run the installer again" for a copy known unfinished, and `installer.team.unrecorded.how.finished` for a finished copy or one the run could not tell (F12, option A; K4094, K4098). **Custody and the release**
- **R19** [met] The Cloudflare access token, and every credential generated or supplied, never appears in any page, log or error text, except R16's panel. Text from the management API is HTML-escaped.
- **R20** [met] The plane's limits are carried from the signed release: the plane bundle of the release R8 chose, hashed against its manifest and verified as R8 says, states the plane's limits (the `limits` of the plane's own configuration, `bio-plane/wrangler.jsonc`), and the install and the update send exactly those, holding no value of their own. The plane states them as exactly one distinct string `bio-plane-limits/1 <key>=<positive integer> …` (one pair per key of `limits`, keys sorted, single spaces), which survives bundling as written. A release whose plane states no limits, or states them unreadably (none, two different statements, or a malformed one), is not used, and is named on the page as R8 names a release that fails verification; when the built-in release states none either, the install or update is refused by name before anything is uploaded, and on the install before anything is created (R4) (K723, K724). - **R21** [met] The install offers the jurisdiction profiles held (`jurisdictions.list`, test profiles excluded), each by name and coverage, with none preselected. An update never changes them.
- **R24** [not yet met] An install never shares another copy's buckets and never overwrites its fleet workers in the same account.

- **R33** [met] The install and the update read back the uploaded script's content and compare its hash with the release; a mismatch is named on the page and no success is claimed (Distribution §5, "byte-verified on read-back"). (K3441) The hash is taken over the uploaded module part's bytes exactly as downloaded (never over a text re-decoding, which alters bytes); and the read-back, like the whole install and update, keeps within a Worker's 128 MB of memory at the release's real size (the 0.85.0 plane was 21.4 MB, the 0.86.0 plane is 29 MB), shown by a test at that size under that bound. (T51; N1240; K3893) At the release's real size the live memory of an install and of an update stays below 112 MB of the 128, at least 16 MB of margin (120 before `installer-steps` R18 held the fleet as hashes), shown by the same test. (T51 fix round; K4176) At 0.86.0's 29 MB the bound is met by holding the release once in each form the act needs: the read-back streamed and hashed incrementally (`installer-steps`' part), and the built-in release held in the installer in the smallest form that still uploads exactly the signed bytes. (K4210, K4234) That form is the signed bytes gzipped and written as base64 (10 MB where the plane is 29 MB), with the two facts the installer reads from the release's text, its limits (R20) and whether it reports builds (R15), taken by the embed step from the verified bytes (R26); the installer expands it once, a few MB at a time, into the one Blob the act uploads, taking the bytes' hash as they come out and refusing the release unless it equals the signed manifest's `sha256`. A repository release is likewise received as a stream into its Blob, its hash taken as the chunks arrive, never gathered whole beside the Blob. The real-size test runs at 29 MB for the install and the update, against the built-in release and a repository release, with the read-back's hash equal byte for byte and a changed byte still named; controls: a built-in release that does not expand to the signed bytes is never uploaded, and a repository release whose bytes do not hash to its manifest is never installed.
