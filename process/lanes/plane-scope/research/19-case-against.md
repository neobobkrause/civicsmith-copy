The case against is **strong** here.
2. **It solves P2 for written imports only.** After the trial 0 UI files are reached from the 15 door and launcher code files (research 15, section 4 [MEASURED]). The case against is **moderate**: the leftover references are of the kind any plug-in design keeps.
3. **Its paper cost is the largest of the five choices.** 163 requirements in 26 modules state or assume today's order (research 17, headline 1 [MEASURED]); by research 17's own classes, 112 of them in 20 modules carry a class other than "generic" or "cite" [MEASURED R3], so they are the ones likely to need rewording (INFERENCE); four rulings or decisions name today's places (research 17, section 3 and "After a change" [READ]); four module-list files and a new module (research 15, section 9 [MEASURED]). **Strong.**

5. **What it gives that nothing else gives:** a program with no UI that builds and runs (25,141,248 bytes, 11.5 % smaller, start-up lower in 8 of 8 rounds by a median 56 ms; research 15, section 7 [MEASURED]), and a door whose code takes its UI as an argument. No user of either was found in the recorded research [NOT CHECKED beyond it]. That benefit is architectural, not yet a product need.
6. **Strongest alternative: option B, with the capture move (d) beside it.** B is the only choice that solves P1; (d) is the cheapest measured step toward P2 (4 files, page script byte-identical, 0 of 91 test files changed; research 16, headline 3 [MEASURED]) and closes the one call of UI code from background work. (c) is worth doing but solves neither P1 nor P2. (b) solves P1 one test at a time and tests a copy of the program.
7. **Bottom line (INFERENCE, section 7):** on the recorded facts the case against re-stacking *now* outweighs the case for it, because it does not answer P1, costs the most text and ownership churn, and lands on code under an open pull request while development is halted. It does not show the re-stacking is wrong: the trial worked, found no behaviour difference in 1,399 tests, and nothing recorded makes it impossible later. B now does not foreclose it.

## 2. The case against the re-stacking

Each argument: the facts, then how strong it is.

### 2.1 It does not solve plane's scope (P1); it adds to the moves P1 already needs

- Plane holds 147 test files; 27 are its own (A1), 27 plane-numbered platform checks of other modules (A2), 86 other modules' proofs (B), 7 odd (D) (research 02, "Grouped counts" [MEASURED by its scripts]). 138 of 147 load plane's own code; 108 start it under workerd (same).
- Its required run took 33 to 43 minutes of a 45-minute limit in four measured runs (research 03; `design.md` section 2, fact 3).
- Under the re-stacked order, 101 of plane's 147 test files are refused where they are; 100 of them reach the top entry; of the 27 A1 files 7 are refused (`worker`, `t42`, `watchdog`, `t47`, `t50`, `r10-arrival-log`, `store`) (research 15, section 8 [MEASURED, static]). 20 of control-plane's 95 are refused: the 12 the trial itself re-pointed to `pl/ui.mjs`, two that start the Worker by its path, five it owns under `interface-shell/`, one holding a journey-check path (same).
- INFERENCE from these: re-stacking alone leaves every whole-program test where it is (at plane or control-plane) and refused, so it must be followed by moving them above the UI, to the top module or to proof modules after it. That is option B's move, with the new top module in plane's place. Beyond B it adds: the door's 20 files, plane's 7 A1 files (B would have kept all 27 A1 at plane), and a top module that today has no tests (research 15, section 10, item 14). Plane's run time is not touched by the re-stacking itself; only the moves shorten it.
- K4990 (1) (`build/rulings.md:4985`, research 17 section 3 [READ, a lead]): "a test never hand-builds another module's answer or a stub of its interface". So the door's page tests cannot stay at the door by handing it a fake UI; they must sit where the real UI is, above it. INFERENCE: the door's own page requirements (control-plane R1, R75, R81, R82, R84, R95, R104 and others of class `serve`, research 17 section 1a) are then proven only above the UI, or reworded as what the door does with what it is handed (research 15, "After a change").

**Strength: strong.** The re-stacking is an answer to P2 only, and its test consequences are a superset of B's.

### 2.2 It leaves references to the UI by the lane's own rule

- Rule 7 counts "a value it registers that is called" (`evidence-rules.md` [READ]). After the trial the door calls `ui.interfaceScriptHash`, `ui.interfacePage`, `ui.pageWork`, `ui.interfaceScript`, `ui.versionOfScriptPath`, `composer.titleOf`, `composer.promoteDocument` and reads `ui.screens`, `ui.guide`, `ui.assets`, `ui.publicAddresses` (research 15, section 4 [MEASURED]). - Untouched by the trial: the shared text ties N01 and N03 to N12 (`/app`, `/app/assets/`, `group-logo`, the options names, the words cache key, `background`, the inline-script rule, storage sentences, a review-copy key, `trace`, answer fields, a bound) (research 11, section 3b [READ]; research 15, "After a change" [READ]).
- `bio-plane/wrangler.jsonc:11` `"main": "src/plane/index.mjs"`, a plane file, names the file the trial gives to the top module, and `scripts/build-plane.mjs` builds from it (research 15, section 4 [READ]). `check` did not refuse it, because it reads imports (research 15, section 8 [MEASURED]).
- Outside the door, the order still has 32 import edges against the wanted stacking (model to middleware 19, shared foundation to model 12, middleware to control plane 1) (research 18 [MEASURED]); and 28 couplings of lower modules to UI things (research 12, part C [READ]). The re-stacking addresses the 13 door-to-UI edges only.

**Strength: moderate.** Handing values in is the normal way to invert a dependency and the process tool does not count it; the text ties are agreements, not uses. But the claim "the door refers to nothing of the UI" would be false as worded under rule 7, and the launcher's config still names a later file.

### 2.4 Its measured risks

- **Load-sensitive restart test.** `plane/l79.test.mjs` "a restart over the same storage" failed in 2 of the changed tree's 3 unspoiled set runs, 0 of the unchanged tree's 2; it passed alone (5 of 5 twice) and in the run of both trees at once; `r8-timing-hook`'s age test failed once (research 15, section 5 [MEASURED]). The trial's INFERENCE is load, "not established: 2 of 3 against 0 of 2 is too few runs". Plane's run is already throughput-bound (research 03). Weak as evidence of a fault; strong as evidence that the change has not been cleared.
- **The served bytes hang on a name.** With the launcher's top-level constant named `doorOver` the bundle served a script 24 characters shorter with a different hash and page `integrity`; naming it `door` restored identical bytes (research 15, section 6 [MEASURED]). The cause is that the page script is function source text (R5) and esbuild renames nested `door` locals in screens when the bundle's top level holds a `door`. No test in the sets noticed the 24 characters (research 15, "After a change"). This hazard exists today too (any new top-level name anywhere in 853 inputs, INFERENCE from R5 and that section); the re-stacking moves the entry's code and so meets it at once.
- **Start order rests on two import lines.** The registration must load before `cp/index.mjs`, `store-request/index.mjs` and two screens modules (research 15, section 3 [MEASURED]); in the trial that is guaranteed by `pl/launch.mjs:10` importing store-assembly first and `pl/index.mjs:6-7` importing the launcher before the UI. Swapping the entry's lines fails loudly; but a test helper that imported `pl/ui.mjs` beside `register-declared.mjs` left the tables at 1,029 ops of 1,068 and nine tests failed, because `test/m/control-plane/register-declared.mjs:14-18` lets that refusal pass (same [MEASURED]). Today the same tie rests on one import line (R4).
- **Unrun tests.** 307 of 3,118 test files reach a changed file; 190 of them were not run after the change, 93 of them plane's (research 15, "What I did not check" [MEASURED, static]). "0 real differences in behaviour" is a statement about 1,399 tests in five sets.
- **Open work.** PR 1259 (plane's first job, ready, 162 files) changes `src/plane/index.mjs` and `plane.json`, which the trial rewrites, and 11 of the 121 refused test files [MEASURED R6]; its job is recorded blocked on the `deliver` hand-over, the seam of T17 (research 17, section 7 [READ]). Development is halted (`decisions-owner.md:38` [MEASURED R7]).
- **Landing under Process4.** "There is no merge of several modules together other than the mechanical edit … the provider first accepts the new form beside the old, the users move, and the provider then drops the old form" (D02 s8, research 17 section 6 [READ]). INFERENCE: the re-stacking can be sequenced (door accepts `hooks.ui` beside its imports; plane hands it in; door drops the imports; ARCH's list change moves the two modules and the tests), but that is at least three jobs and one list change, none trialled as a sequence. Not shown impossible; shown to be several rounds.

**Strength: moderate.** No risk recorded is a demonstrated fault of the change; several are demonstrated fragilities it touches.

### 2.5 What the re-stacking gives that the case against must concede

- The code change is small and works: 6 product files, +170 -107, 59 of the added lines moved as they were (research 15, section 9 [MEASURED]); control-plane 564 of 564 after 14 test files' set-up changed; interface-shell 321 of 321, screens-intake 375 of 375 unchanged; served bytes for 15 requests the same under workerd (research 15, sections 5 and 6 [MEASURED]).

- The owner asked for this order (`evidence-rules.md` [READ]). That carries weight beyond the facts; it is not a recorded decision (R7).

**So the case against is weak on feasibility** (it can be done, and done safely as far as measured) **and strong on value now** (it does not solve P1, and its benefits have no recorded user).
